GRPO with only half the push

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct · LoRA · GSM8K · full vs positive-only vs negative-only GRPO · 2 seeds each

Code and README on GitHub

Summary

GRPO samples a group of answers per question, then pushes each answer up if it beat the group average and down if it fell below. Three versions were trained:

  •   Full GRPO: both pushes.
  •   Positive-only: only the up-push (mostly right answers).
  •   Negative-only: only the down-push (mostly wrong answers).

Greedy: one answer per question, always the most likely token. 1 try and 64 tries: share of the 1,319 test questions solved at least once in k answers sampled at temperature 1 (pass@1, pass@64). Arm values are the mean of two seeds.

  • Both pushes are needed. Full GRPO is best at every number of tries, in both seeds.
  • Mostly sharpening. The gain over the original is +16 points at 1 try but +2.8 at 64 tries.
  • The halves behave in opposite ways but end up tied on what the model can solve. Positive-only shortens answers and negative-only lengthens them, yet from 16 tries on they're equal. The narrowing effect reported by Zhu et al. (2025) did not appear here.

2Inside the 64 tries

How many of 64 answers are right, per question

Full GRPO moves the most questions to "always right" (5–6% at 64/64 vs 0.1% for the original). Positive-only raises the middle but almost never reaches 64/64. Every run shrinks the "never right" band (8.5% → 5.6–7.8%).

Questions gained and lost

Against the original, which solves 1,207 of 1,319 questions at least once in 64 tries. Lost = the original sometimes solved it, the run never did. Gained = the reverse.

Every run gains more than it loses. The losses are systematic: the two seeds of an arm lose the same 13–18 questions, where about 1 would be shared by chance.

4Caveats

  • Small setting. One 0.5B model, one benchmark, 200 updates, two seeds per arm. The original already solves 91.5% of questions within 64 tries, leaving little room for the solvable set to grow or shrink. Zhu et al. (2025) used larger models (up to 8B) and harder math, so this is not evidence against their result.
  • Negative-only seed 1 broke. 45.6% of its sampled answers hit the 512-token limit. Strict scoring still credits a boxed answer written before the cut-off. Counting only answers that stopped on their own, its pass@1 drops from 43.6% to 30.2%, below the original's 33.6%. The other runs change by at most 0.3 points.
  • Part of the gain is formatting. Only \boxed{} or #### answers count. 19.9% of the original's sampled answers end with an unformatted number, against 1.6–4.7% after training. About 3 of the 16-point pass@1 gain comes from this.

Setup: LoRA rank 16, alpha 32; 16 question groups × 8 answers per update; AdamW, learning rate 1e-4; KL coefficient 0.02; temperature 1.0; up to 512 new tokens; 200 updates. All seven models share the same per-question sampling seeds. References: Shao et al. 2024 (GRPO) · Zhu et al. 2025 · Yue et al. 2025 · Mirzadeh et al. 2024 (GSM-Symbolic).
